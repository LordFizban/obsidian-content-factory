## 🧠 Phase 1: Spark & Roast

### Agile Coach Roast

**The raw material:**
- Tech lead complains testers "test beyond what's required" and expand scope.
- Tester brings up shallow impact analysis in retro (she catches gaps downstream). It falls on silent ground because nobody understands what she means.
- The SM's "perception": Both are reacting to immature acceptance criteria and incomplete definition of done.
- The SM's solution: Invite the tester upstream into pre-refinement (with Tech Lead, Senior Analyst, PO) so she can share her perspective before stories hit the team. She says she is busy but will try to attend.
- The SM also notes the need to revise AC and DoD.

**Strength assessment:**
- ✅ Two voices, one problem — high structural novelty
- ✅ Concrete, specific, anonymizable
- ✅ Bridges MP (facilitating the conversation) and CI (improving DoD/AC)
- ✅ Musashi quote slots perfectly — perception vs sight is literally the concept page title
- ✅ The "reveal" moment (both complaints point to the same root cause) creates a strong curiosity payoff

**Psychology Stack:**
- **Blind Spot Bias:** Each team member sees the other's flaw but not their own contribution to the system.
- **Fundamental Attribution Error:** Tech lead attributes the problem to the tester's behavior. Tester attributes it to the dev's carelessness. Both are attributing to people, not to the system.
- **Contrast Effect:** The two complaints, presented side by side, create a "wait, those are the same thing" moment.
- **Information Asymmetry (Curiosity Gap):** Reader wonders "what's the SM seeing that they're not?"

## 📝 Phase 2: Polished Draft — LinkedIn Post

```
My tech lead told me the tester was testing beyond what was required.

That same week, the tester raised an issue during our retro. She explained that the developers weren't doing proper impact analysis, and she was catching all their gaps during the testing phase. Her complaint fell on completely silent ground. Nobody else understood what she meant, and the conversation just moved on.

Two people had brought up opposite complaints in the space of a few days.

But I wasn't listening for who was right. I was listening for what they were both describing without knowing it.

The tech lead saw a tester who was expanding scope and testing things nobody had asked for. The tester saw developers who were shipping work with downstream surprises. They were looking at the exact same gap from different angles.

Miyamoto Musashi wrote: "Perception is strong and sight is weak. In strategy it is important to see distant things as if they were close and to take a distanced view of close things."

Sight is the complaint in front of you. Perception is the pattern underneath it.

The acceptance criteria were too thin and the definition of done was incomplete. Neither side had a shared picture of what "finished" actually looked like, so each person filled that gap with their own standard.

My solution wasn't to arbitrate the dispute. I invited the tester to join our pre-refinement sessions with the tech lead, senior analyst, and product owner. Instead of catching gaps at the end, she could specify the impacts we were losing sight of before the user stories even reached the team.

She told me she is busy but will attend if she has time. Meanwhile, I noted down that we have structural work to do to revise both our acceptance criteria and our definition of done.

What's one recurring complaint on your team that might be describing the same problem as another complaint, just from the other side?
```
