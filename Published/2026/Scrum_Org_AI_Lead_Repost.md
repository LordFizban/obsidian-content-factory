### 📝 Post Summary

A "Lighthouse" style repost of a Scrum.org Community Podcast post (featuring Eric Naiburg and Darrell Fernandes). The original post asked if Scrum Masters are the new "AI Leads."

The repost caption grounds this concept in reality by referencing the internal use of the **StoryReady** AI tool to test assumptions before Sprint Planning, and explicitly delineates the accountability:

- PO owns AI in the product.
- SM owns AI in the operational workflow.

---

### 📱 The Caption

Eric Naiburg (Scrum.org COO) and Darrell Fernandes make a fair point on the Scrum.org Community Podcast: the Scrum Master role is shifting. But what does "leading the AI charge" actually look like on the ground?

It doesn’t mean becoming a prompt engineering expert.
It doesn’t mean shoving new tools at a team that's already busy.

When a team is worried about AI changing how they work, pushing back is natural. Our job is to create enough safety for them to test the waters.

For example, when we noticed our Definition of Ready was slipping and tickets were too vague, we didn't just add more process. Instead, we ran an experiment: using an internal AI tool (StoryReady) to pressure-test our assumptions *before* Sprint Planning. We treated it like a sparring partner.

🛑 Product Owners look at how to build AI into the product.
🟢 Scrum Masters look at how to build AI into the team's workflow.

Don't shield your team from AI. Give them the space to argue with it and figure out what works.

For the other SMs out there: how are you introducing AI into your team's daily practices? Let me know below. 👇
