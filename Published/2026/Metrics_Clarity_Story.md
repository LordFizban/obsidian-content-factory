# LinkedIn Mini-Post Draft: Time-to-Clarity

**Scheduled:** Tuesday, January 27, 2026 @ 09:00 TRT
**Pillar:** Metrics That Matter
**Format:** Text Post (Mini-Content)

---

## 📝 Post Text

Stop tracking how much your team works.
Start tracking how long they wait.

Last week, a frontend dev couldn't start until Day 10 of a 3-week sprint.
Not because he was slow.
Because he was waiting.

Waiting for screenshots.
Waiting for field specifications.
Waiting for someone to tell him *what* was actually needed.

The requirements were in the Definition of Ready.
But he'd stopped asking.

"I gave up," he told me. "No one answers."

Here's the thing: We obsess over velocity.
But velocity measures what gets *done*.
It doesn't measure what gets *stuck*.

If your team is "slow," don't look at their output.
Look at their wait time.

I told him: "When we started this journey, our acceptance criteria were one line long. Now they're mature. That change didn't happen fast. It happened because we challenged our assumptions over and over. Like an ocean wave—relentless."

He got it. He said he'd start asking again.

The bottleneck isn't your team's speed.
It's the time between "I need this" and "Here it is."

What's the longest your team has waited for clarity this sprint?
