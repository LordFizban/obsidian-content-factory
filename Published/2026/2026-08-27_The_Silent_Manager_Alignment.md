# The Silent Manager Alignment

## Post Body

A deadline was slipping. Nobody came to me. The manager went straight to a developer on the team.

That is not an exception. That is the pattern. In every escalation I can remember, the manager has gone directly to the person doing the work. The Scrum Master is simply not part of that decision chain.

By the time I heard about it, the conversation was already over. The developer had stayed late, rearranged their priorities, and absorbed the pressure in silence.

The sprint technically stayed on track. But something shifted.

The developer who used to challenge assumptions in refinement and push back on estimates sat silent through the entire retro. They were still delivering code, but the spark was gone.

My first instinct was to set a boundary: tell her to route requests through me. But that misses the point. Her authority runs through direct individual control. Rerouting through me does not empower the team; it diminishes her. And a manager who feels diminished does not collaborate, they escalate.

In *𝘛𝘶𝘳𝘯 𝘵𝘩𝘦 𝘚𝘩𝘪𝘱 𝘈𝘳𝘰𝘶𝘯𝘥!*, **𝗗𝗮𝘃𝗶𝗱 𝗠𝗮𝗿𝗾𝘂𝗲𝘁** diagnosed this exact operating model on the USS Santa Fe: the **𝗹𝗲𝗮𝗱𝗲𝗿-𝗳𝗼𝗹𝗹𝗼𝘄𝗲𝗿 𝘁𝗿𝗮𝗽**. When authority requires direct control, people stop thinking independently.

The Marquet move would be coaching the developer to state intent ("I can look at this after our sprint commitment, or here is what moves off the board"). But pushing intent language into an established hierarchy without clear alignment feels reckless.

So I am sitting with the tension. Both sides are running on leader-follower software, and seeing it does not mean knowing how to fix it immediately.

Three questions I keep coming back to:

**𝟭)** When an escalation happens, are you involved, or do you find out after the fact?

**𝟮)** Has a vocal developer on your team quietly stopped speaking up?

**𝟯)** If you could coach one person here, is it the manager or the developer?

📌 Save this for the next time an escalation bypasses your sprint.
