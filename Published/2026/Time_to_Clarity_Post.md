## 📝 Post Text

Five days. That's how much tester capacity we lost last week.
Not to bad work. To a vendor dependency.

It started small. A test build due Friday arrived at end of day. One day gone.

Monday: the build couldn't connect to our test server.
An unauthorised token. Nobody knew why.

Our tech lead suspected a security config change. I escalated to engineering.
Infra assumed network — I disagreed. One token working and another failing isn't a network problem.
Engineering confirmed the tech lead's hunch. Fixed by Tuesday afternoon.

Three days gone. The testers finally started.
The app crashed.

Vendor shipped an overnight fix.
Wednesday morning: more crashes.

I pulled up the vendor's product backlog.
They'd taken on far more features than this build could handle.

The team self-organized a call with the Business Owner to discuss the situation.
I joined to observe — and to ask one question:
"Did we request all these features for this release?"

No.

Afterward, I sat with the Business Owner one-on-one.
I showed her what I saw in the vendor's backlog: they were over-committing.
We discussed options.

By Friday, the app still crashed.
Five days. A full week of tester capacity — gone.

Not because our team was slow.
Because our vendor tried to deliver everything and delivered nothing.

When your Definition of Done depends on someone outside your team, your sprint isn't really yours.

What's the most time your team has lost to a vendor dependency lately?
