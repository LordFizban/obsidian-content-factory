# The Same Problem, From Different Floors

Two people came to me this week about the same problem.

Neither of them knew it.

First, a Key Business Responsible pulled me aside. She was frustrated.

"I have explained these requirements three times. The team still doesn't get it. These meetings feel pointless."

Two days later — a message from the Business Owner. Different tone. Same energy.

"We need to clarify responsibilities between business and IT."

One complaint was emotional.
The other was structural.
Both pointed to the same root cause.

The handoff between the old PO and the new PO had left gaps. There was no clear Definition of Ready. Roles were blurring — the KBR was even getting assigned test tickets that weren't hers.

I didn't schedule two meetings. I scheduled one.

Then on Friday, the PO messaged me.

"The business team refuses to discuss a user story during refinement. They say they'll clarify it in our meeting. I'm bringing in an analyst to cover it."

I told her no.

This is not a requirements meeting. This is a facilitation space — where we take feedback from all sides and decide what needs to change in how we operate.

I don't step into rooms to clarify requirements. That's refinement. That stays with the PO.

If I step in anywhere, it is because I see a problem in the flow.

The meeting hasn't happened yet.
But the boundary has been set.

A Scrum Master who solves requirements problems isn't facilitating.

They're compensating.
