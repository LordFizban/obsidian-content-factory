## Draft (Option 1 — Selected)

A Japanese swordsman, a Japanese novelist, and a DevOps engineer walk into my reading list.

These are the three books shaping how I lead this quarter.

📖 The Book of Five Rings — Miyamoto Musashi

Haven't started this one yet. It's next.

Here's what I know: Musashi fought over 60 duels and never lost a single one.

They call this book a strategy classic — Japan's answer to The Art of War.

But what caught me was the back cover:
"The process of struggle and mastery over conflict that underlies every level of human interaction."

Every level of human interaction.

That's not a sword fight. That's a Sprint Review.

I carry a framework I call the Warrior vs. the Statesman.
The Warrior solves by force. The Statesman changes the system.

I want to know what a man who never lost would say about that distinction.

Maybe he'd say the real fight is choosing which one to be.

📖 1Q84 — Haruki Murakami

Just started this one.

Murakami writes about characters living in parallel worlds — same city, different realities.

I'm only a few chapters in, but I already see it.

The team sees "we delivered 8 stories."
The business owner sees "the feature I needed isn't here."

Same sprint. Two different worlds.

My job isn't to pick a side.
It's to build the bridge.

📖 The Phoenix Project — Gene Kim (re-read)

The original "business novel as learning tool."

I first read this 3 years ago. I'm revisiting it now because my team is drowning in WIP.

Back then I highlighted the Theory of Constraints.
This time I'm highlighting something else:

The character Brent.

Every team has a Brent — the one person everything depends on.

My question this quarter:
Who is our Brent, and what happens when he takes a vacation?

Three books. Three lenses.

A swordsman for mastery.
A novelist for perspective.
An engineer for patterns.

What's on your reading list this quarter?
