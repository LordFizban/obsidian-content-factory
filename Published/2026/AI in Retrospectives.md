# When Your Agile Coach is Unavailable, AI Becomes Your Sparring Partner

![Agile Sparring Partner](ai_sparring_partner_header.png)

My Agile Coach was unavailable. The retrospective was in three days. And for the first time, I had to design and facilitate the whole thing myself.

In the past, coaches always prepared the format. I just showed up. But this time, with two new team members joining and a specific learning goal (vertical slicing), I needed something special. So I did what any modern Scrum Master would do: I asked AI.

## The "Safe Mode" Trap

When experts are unavailable, we often default to "playing it safe"—reusing old formats, postponing the hard conversations, or running a mediocre session just to check the box.

But I love learning, so I decided to partner with AI here.

And I noticed something different: it did not just give me answers. It challenged my assumptions, offered alternatives I had never considered, and forced me to think deeper about *why* I wanted what I wanted.

The pattern is this: **AI works best not as a replacement for expertise, but as a thinking accelerator when expertise is temporarily out of reach.**

## The "Style Over Substance" Warning

Here is what surprised me: the AI did not just suggest retrospective formats. It *critiqued* my instincts.

When I mentioned my team loves gamification, it warned me: "Gamification carries a risk—style over substance. If a team loves games, they might prioritize the *fun* of the retrospective over the *pain* of the actual improvement."

That single sentence reframed my entire approach. I was not looking for a fun game; I was looking for a game that *teaches*.

The result? A visual D&D-style board tailored giving us a totally new language for team dynamics:

![D&D Retro Board](dnd_retro_board_concept.png)

It featured **RPG Stats** for introductions, an **Exhaustion Meter** for safe burnout signaling, and **Quest Slots** to gamify our Kaizen goals.

The AI helped me build it step-by-step in Miro—from token mechanics to layer locking to the exact Turkish facilitation script.

## The Retro: From Report to "Party"

The retrospective ran for two hours instead of the planned one. Why? Because the format unlocked something unexpected.

Two new members finally felt they "knew" everyone. Old team members dived deep into personal struggles, and others stepped up to offer life advice. The dynamic shifted from "reporting status" to "building a party."

Here is what emerged from the data:

**The "Tank" Problem:** Our Product Owner scored himself 9/9/6 across adaptability, resilience, and knowledge. The AI had predicted this—when one person carries everything, the team cannot self-organize. We now have an action to redistribute decision-making to our Tech Lead.

**The "Culture Clash":** One new member kept using formal titles ("Bey/Hanim"). She came from the public sector. The AI advised me not to force informality—let her keep her "armor" until she feels safe. Psychological safety cannot be commanded; it must be demonstrated.

**The "Mercenary":** A shared team member rated his domain knowledge at 2/10. He only joins when needed and loses context every time. His "Quest"? "Become self-sufficient." We are assigning him the onboarding documentation—he learns while improving it for others.

None of this would have surfaced in a "Start-Stop-Continue" format.

## The Tradeoff

*When your expert is unavailable, do you postpone the hard work—or do you find a new sparring partner?*
