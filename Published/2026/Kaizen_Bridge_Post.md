## 📄 Draft Post (Option 1)

```
I'm a Scrum Master in healthcare insurance.

Last week, I read Mark Graban's article on Kaizen in healthcare.
It hit me: my retros were "suggestion boxes"—not problem-solving.

Here's what I stole:

1️⃣ **Kaizen = daily improvement.**
   Retros are every 3 weeks. That's a massive gap.

2️⃣ **Leaders as coaches, not bosses.**
   Graban says Kaizen requires managers to be "partners in improvement."
   Sound familiar? That's Servant Leadership.

3️⃣ **No suggestion boxes.**
   Kaizen uses the scientific method (PDCA).
   So should our retro actions—test, learn, adapt.

Earlier this year, I prompted an AI for retro ideas.
I expected a template. Instead, I got a roast.

"Gamification without depth is distraction," it warned.

It didn't just suggest formats. It *critiqued* my instincts.
It asked the hard questions my ego was avoiding.

That's Kaizen thinking: challenge the facilitator, not just the team.

Scrum borrowed more from Lean than we admit.
Maybe it's time we borrowed back.
```
