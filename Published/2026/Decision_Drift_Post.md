# Decision Drift: A Scrum Master's Perspective

We made the right call. Two weeks later, we'd already undone it.

My team's testing was always in pain. We hit stable velocity, but quality suffered at the edges.

So we ran an experiment: plan for slightly less velocity. Give testing room to breathe.

Week 1: everything went as planned.

Week 2: the team found a fix for a problem blocking the Sprint Goal. They pulled it in. Reasonable, right?

Here's what nobody noticed:

By adding that item, we were back at our average velocity. The exact number we were trying to escape.

The decision didn't fail. Nobody disagreed with the experiment. It just quietly dissolved under one perfectly rational exception.

Danish Soomro calls this "Decision Drift"—good decisions that lose their force. Not through rebellion. Through accommodation.

I pointed it out. The team got together and dropped an item to honor the original commitment.

That's the Scrum Master's job in one moment:
Not making the decision.
Noticing when it's silently dying.

When did you last check if a decision your team "agreed on" is still alive?
