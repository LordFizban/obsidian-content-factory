## 🧠 Phase 1: Spark & Roast

### Agile Coach Roast
- **The Article:** Jurgen Appelo argues the Org Chart substitutes coordination for work. It promotes people who are best at "coordinating the coordination" until the people actually doing the craft are exhausted and checked out.
- **First-pass flaw:** The original draft opened with a hypothetical meeting scenario ("I spent three hours yesterday in a meeting about..."). This violates Rule 4 (concrete sprint moment) and reduces Authenticity. The SM has never been in a 3-hour visioning alignment meeting. It sounds fabricated.
- **The Fix:** Ground it in a real observation from the SM's teams. The SM has seen the "chart choking the craft" in action when pre-refinement, refinement, backlog grooming, and sprint planning all cover overlapping ground, and the developers spend more time talking about work than doing work.
- **The Statesman Move:** The SM doesn't need to blow up the org chart. The Statesman move is to audit which ceremonies serve the craft and which have become "coordination of the coordination."
- **Psychology Stack:**
    - **Calendar Density Bias:** We feel productive when our calendar is full, even if we ship zero value.
    - **Sunk Cost Fallacy:** "We've always had this meeting" keeps zombie ceremonies alive.
    - **Loss Aversion:** Managers fear losing oversight (the chart) more than they value the time their team gets back.

## 📝 Phase 2: Polished Draft — LinkedIn Post

```
Last week I counted the hours my developers spent in ceremonies versus the hours they spent actually writing code. The ratio was uncomfortable.

Pre-refinement, refinement, backlog grooming, sprint planning, daily standup, sprint review, retrospective. Every single one of these exists for a reason. But when I lined them up on a calendar, I realized some of them were covering the same ground. We were coordinating the coordination.

Jurgen Appelo recently wrote that the org chart was built for a world where coordination was expensive and execution was cheap. He calls it "the org chart choking your craft." We hire intelligent adults for the things they can do that most people can't, and then we fill their calendars with alignment sessions until the craft sits in a corner waiting for permission to work.

I felt that one personally, because I am one of the people filling those calendars.

As a Scrum Master, every ceremony I facilitate is supposed to serve the team's ability to ship work that matters. But if I'm honest, I've kept meetings alive longer than they needed to live because "we've always had this meeting" felt safer than "let's cut it and see what happens."

Jurgen's challenge is worth sitting with: if your team spent more time this week talking about work than doing work, the chart might be winning. And sometimes the person holding the chart is you.

What is one recurring meeting on your calendar that you suspect exists more out of habit than necessity?
```
