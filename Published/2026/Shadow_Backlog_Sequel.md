## 🧠 Phase 1: Agile Coach Roast

> **Assessment:** This is the perfect follow-up to the W18 CI post. The W18 post was about *setting* the rules (Continuous Improvement). This post is about *sustaining* them (Manager Partnership / Trust). The core insight is brilliant: "Process doesn't enforce process, relationships do." It pivots from the mechanical (the 4 rules) to the human (the bonds).
>
> **Psychology Stack:** 
> - **Pratfall Effect:** Admitting that a junior SM would take the credit for the "process" working, while the senior SM knows it's the invisible work.
> - **The Halo Effect:** The trust built in individual relationships transfers into trust for the new process.
> - **Open Loop:** "Nobody has broken them. Here is why you shouldn't be impressed."

## 📝 Phase 2: Polished Draft — LinkedIn Post

```
We set four ground rules to kill our shadow backlog last week.

No more direct requests to developers. Everything goes through the Product Owner first.

I have been watching the team for the last seven days.
Zero divergences. Everyone is following the new process.

If I were a junior Scrum Master, I would update my Jira dashboard, declare victory, and attribute the success to my excellent facilitation.

But process doesn't enforce process.

The reason those four rules held isn't because they were written in a Confluence page. 
It is because of the conversations that happened after the meeting.

I spent the last week quietly checking in with the individuals I had spent months building bonds with.
"How is the new flow feeling?"
"Is the PO becoming a bottleneck?"
"Tell me the truth—are you still getting side-channel requests?"

Rules give you a baseline. But relationships enforce the rules.

When people trust you, they don't just agree to a process in a meeting. 
They tell you when the process starts to chafe, long before they break it.

You can write the most elegant Definition of Ready in the world.
If you don't have the relational capital to ask "how is this really working?" behind closed doors, your process is just a PDF nobody reads.

*(PS: Missed the story of how we set those four rules? Read it here: https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:share:7454111496957132800)*

What's a process your team follows not because it's documented, but because they trust the people behind it?
```
