# LinkedIn Post Body

A new Product Owner joined my team last week.

She told me she was anxious — first time in the role, zero knowledge transfer from the previous PO, and a team she had never worked with before.

On her very first day, I did something counterintuitive:

I opened a private channel with her instead of helping her in the team group chat.

Here's why.

If the team sees the Scrum Master publicly coaching the PO, they don't see a leader finding her footing.

They see someone who needs rescuing.

And a PO who starts with a "rescue" label never fully shakes it off.

So I chose to make her stronger privately.

When a team member asked her to create a user story for removing tech debt — I almost jumped in. Almost told everyone that any developer can draft a story too.

I didn't.

Later that day, she messaged me: "Who's actually allowed to register stories in the backlog?"

I pointed her back to that exact moment.

"Anyone on the team can create stories — with your consent and approval. The previous PO didn't allow it. It gave her control, but it bottlenecked her over time. You get to decide what kind of PO you want to be."

Then something happened that made the whole approach worth it.

In our shared channel with the Tech Lead, she started questioning whether a story delivered real user value — and whether it should be split into smaller pieces.

The Tech Lead answered her.

No escalation to me. No triangulation. Just two critical roles working in unison because nobody forced them together.

At the end of her first week, I sent her a private message:

"Your first week was tough. But the way you handled it — your mindset is exactly where it needs to be."

She replied:

"I'm doing the best I can, using logic as my anchor."

That line told me everything.

She doesn't need rescuing.

She needs space.
