## 🧠 Phase -1 & 0: Spark & Roast

> **The Roast:**
> Esther Derby nails the exact trap many Tech Leads, Managers, and Scrum Masters fall into: the "Dependency Dynamic." A manager tells the team to self-organize, the team hesitates (because they aren't used to it), and the manager grows impatient and jumps in to solve the problem for them. In trying to be helpful, the leader accidentally proves to the team that "empowerment" was a lie, and their true job is just to wait for the leader to decide.
> 
> **The Canon:**
> - **Esther's Core Insight:** People respond to the system. Adults who manage mortgages and raise kids aren't inherently "dependent." The work environment manufactured that dependency. 
> - **Kaan's Synthesis (Lighthouse Leadership):** The Warrior leader jumps in to fill the silence. The Statesman (the Lighthouse) holds the silence. The Statesman knows that letting the team struggle through the hesitation is the only way to build muscle.

## 📄 Phase 2b: Draft Post 

```
He told his developers they were empowered. 
Ten minutes later, he was assigning their tickets.

Esther Derby writes brilliantly about this trap. She calls it the "Dependency Dynamic." 

It usually goes exactly like this:
A leader decides they want the team to be more autonomous. They step back and tell the team to self-organize.

The team, used to being told what to do, hesitates. They look at the leader. They wait.

The silence stretches. The leader feels the awkwardness. They get impatient.
And because they want to "help," they jump back in and make the decision.

In that single moment, the team learns the truth:
"Empowerment" was just a phase. If we wait long enough, the leader will do the work.

We wonder why highly capable adults—people who negotiate mortgages, manage investments, and raise children—suddenly act helpless at work.

They aren't helpless. They are just responding to the system we created. 

If you want a self-organizing team, you have to be willing to sit through the silence. You have to let them struggle with the hesitation. 

The Warrior jumps in to save the day.
The Statesman holds the space until the team saves itself.

When was the last time you accidentally rescued a team that just needed more time to think?
```
