## 📝 Phase 2: Polished Draft — LinkedIn Post

```
I had a fever the day I was supposed to facilitate the meeting that would fix three sprints of confusion.

I didn't postpone it.

Two complaints had landed on my desk independently.
One from business, one from management.
Different words. Same root cause.

When I brought everyone into the same room, the pattern surfaced in minutes:

A key business contact was sending requests directly to developers.
No Product Owner in the loop.
No priority check.
No sprint impact assessment.

The team was building features based on requirements that weren't complete.
One item sat finished for weeks — built perfectly, unable to close.
Why? The business input it depended on didn't exist yet.

Nobody was wrong.
But nobody could see the full picture either.

We didn't leave that room until we had four agreements:

1. New requests go to the business owner first — priority and impact before anything moves.
2. The first conversation is with the Product Owner, not the developers — what comes in, what goes out.
3. When a requirement depends on an unknown variable, the team builds the infrastructure. When the variable arrives, a new item is linked.
4. Business contacts align among themselves before raising a request.

None of these were revolutionary.
But none of them had been said out loud before.

The invisible backlog disappeared the moment we made it visible.

That's continuous improvement.
Not a retro action item.
Not a Jira filter.
A conversation that should have happened three sprints ago.

What's the one process gap in your team that everyone works around but nobody talks about?
```
