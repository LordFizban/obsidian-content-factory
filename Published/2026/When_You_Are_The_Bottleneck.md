### Psychology Stack

- **Single Point of Failure Illusion:** We don't see the dependency until the node goes offline.
- **Protector's Paradox:** The PO's protective instinct ("I'll handle this so the team can focus") creates the vulnerability he's trying to prevent.
- **Bystander Effect (inverted):** When the PO always handles it, the team learns not to learn.

## 📝 Phase 2: Polished Draft — LinkedIn Post

```
Our Product Owner handled the email templates himself. Every time a business rule changed, he opened the system, made the adjustment, and moved on. Half a day of work. He did it to protect the team from one more context switch.

Then he went on vacation.

Three days into the sprint, a template change came in. The tech lead started digging through the system, trying to figure out how the PO had been doing it. The business kept pushing: this should be simple, why is it taking so long?

Two days passed. The key stakeholder escalated to management in writing, on calls, everywhere she could.

A task that took the PO half a day had stalled the team for two full days.

When the PO came back, I sat him down and walked through what happened. The escalation, the tech lead's frustration at being pressured by the business while learning a process nobody had documented. I told him he needed to delegate.

He started to say he was protecting them. I told him protection without documentation is just a different kind of risk.

He talked to the tech lead. He heard what those two days felt like from the other side. He agreed to hand over the email templates.

In The Phoenix Project, Gene Kim writes about Brent: the engineer who becomes a constraint because he's too good at his job to share. But Brent was indispensable because of skill. Our PO was indispensable because of permission. Nobody touched the templates, not because they couldn't, but because he never let them.

He delegated the email templates after that. But other tasks he'd been handling alone stayed hidden. One conversation didn't cure the pattern.

Next Monday, sit down with your PO and list every task only they can do. If the list has more than two items, you don't have a resilient team. You have a dependency waiting to break.
```
