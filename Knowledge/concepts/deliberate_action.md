# Deliberate Action (Pause & Voice)

## Definition
A operational safety ritual introduced by Captain L. David Marquet on the *USS Santa Fe* where crew members pause, point at a physical control, and voice their intended action out loud before executing it. This interrupts autopilot muscle memory, surfaces unexamined assumptions, and prevents high-stakes operational errors.

## Current Rule
**Rule 41 (Proposed):** Institute a "Deliberate Action" pause during production deployments or sprint review config changes: have the engineer voice their exact intent and expected outcome before hitting execute.

## Evolution
- **2026-08-02:** Ingested from David Marquet's *Turn the Ship Around!*. Designated as a core Psychological Safety / Operational Excellence ritual.

## Counter-Arguments & Data Gaps
1. **Against:** Deliberate action can feel bureaucratic or performative to senior engineers during routine, low-risk daily tasks.
2. **Data Gap:** Need post analytics comparing a "submarine safety ritual" story against a standard DevOps deployment post.
3. **Bias Check:** Ensure deliberate action does not create fear of taking initiative or slow down low-risk experimentation.
