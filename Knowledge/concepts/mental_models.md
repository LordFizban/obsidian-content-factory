# Mental Models

## Definition

Mental models are deeply ingrained assumptions, generalizations, internal pictures, or cognitive shortcuts that determine how we interpret reality and how we take action. As Peter Senge articulates in *The Fifth Discipline* (Chapter 10), organizations frequently fail to implement systemic, high-leverage strategies not because intentions or technical skills are lacking, but because new strategies directly clash with unspoken, unexamined mental models held by managers and teams.

Managing mental models involves two primary disciplines:
1. **Reflective Practice (Surfacing & Examining):** Bringing tacit assumptions to the surface so they can be examined, tested, and updated.
2. **Action Science in Conversation (Inquiry & Advocacy):** Shifting team communication from defensive positioning to collaborative discovery.

---

## Core Frameworks & Mechanisms (The Senge & Argyris Lineage)

### 1. Leaps of Abstraction & The Ladder of Inference
The human brain instinctively jumps from concrete observable data directly to generalized evaluations without pausing to test the reasoning. When an engineer misses a sprint demo, team members often make a leap of abstraction: *"They don't care about the product"* rather than *"They encountered a blocker or emergency."* Once formed, generalizations are treated as raw facts, creating defensive barriers.

### 2. The Left-Hand Column (LHC) Exercise
Originated by Chris Argyris, this technique reveals how managers manipulate conversations to avoid vulnerability:
- **Right-Hand Column:** What was actually said in the meeting.
- **Left-Hand Column:** What the participant was secretly thinking and feeling but chose not to voice.
Analyzing the LHC exposes defensive routines, polite silence, and the unspoken friction stalling organizational change.

### 3. Balancing Inquiry and Advocacy
Most corporate discussions are dominated by pure advocacy (selling a viewpoint, arguing to win, defending territory). A learning organization balances strong advocacy with active inquiry:
- **Advocacy:** *"Here is my view and the data/logic that led me to it."*
- **Inquiry:** *"Here is where my reasoning might be incomplete. What data do you have that contradicts this? Where do you see gaps in my mental model?"*

### 4. Espoused Theory vs. Theory-in-Use
- **Espoused Theory:** The values and policies an organization claims to uphold (e.g., *"We encourage failure and psychological safety"*).
- **Theory-in-Use:** The actual mental model governing behavior (e.g., *"If your experiment breaks staging, your quarterly review is penalized"*).
True coaching closes the gap between espoused theory and theory-in-use.

## Scrum Master & Agile Leadership Application

- **Manager Partnership (MP):** When middle management resists iterative releases, diagnose their underlying mental model (*"Big-bang delivery feels safe; incremental delivery looks chaotic"*). Rather than arguing about Scrum terminology, use scenario planning and shared inquiry to test their assumptions against real sprint data.
- **Sprint Retrospectives (Team Learning):** Combat retro fatigue by introducing the Left-Hand Column or Ladder of Inference. Transform retrospective debates from personal blame into an objective examination of the team's collective mental models.
- **Warrior vs. Statesman Transition:** The Warrior fights individual symptoms and attempts to enforce process compliance by sheer willpower. The Statesman works at the level of mental models—surfacing the unspoken assumptions that cause systemic bottlenecks.

## Current Rule

**Rule 43 (Proposed):** When encountering persistent organizational friction or resistance to agile adoption, pause tactical process enforcement. First surface and map the leadership team's unexamined mental model (using the Ladder of Inference and Inquiry vs. Advocacy) before introducing new ceremonies or metrics.

## Counter-Arguments & Data Gaps

1. **Against:** Focusing heavily on unearthing mental models can devolve into corporate pop-psychology or paralyzed analysis ("navel-gazing") if not tightly coupled with tangible delivery metrics, working software, and concrete sprint artifacts.
2. **Data Gap:** We need quantitative tracking on whether content highlighting cognitive frameworks (Ladder of Inference, Left-Hand Column) drives higher Save rates and DM inquiries among senior engineering managers compared to tactical Scrum mechanics.
3. **Bias Check:** Avoid assuming all management pushback stems from flawed mental models. Frequently, executive pushback is grounded in hard fiscal, compliance, or regulatory constraints that the Scrum team simply lacks visibility into.
