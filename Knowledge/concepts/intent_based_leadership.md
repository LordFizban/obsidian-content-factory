# Intent-Based Leadership ("I Intend To...")

## Definition
A leadership practice where team members replace permission-seeking ("May I do X?" or "What should I do?") with intent-stating ("I intend to do X because Y"). The leader shifts from giving orders to asking clarifying questions and acknowledging intent ("Very well").

## Current Rule
**Rule 38 (Proposed):** Coach team members and Product Owners to state intent ("I intend to slice this story into 2 tasks") rather than asking Scrum Masters for permission. The SM validates clarity and competence rather than prescribing solutions.

## Evolution
- **2026-08-02:** Ingested from David Marquet's *Turn the Ship Around!*. Anchored as a core facilitation pattern for Psychological Safety and Manager Partnership.

## Counter-Arguments & Data Gaps
1. **Against:** In low-trust corporate environments or with junior developers who lack domain context, jumping straight to "I intend to" can lead to costly unreviewed mistakes if competence isn't verified first.
2. **Data Gap:** Need post performance data testing an explicit "I intend to" submarine hook against a standard agile delegation post.
3. **Bias Check:** Ensure "I intend to" does not become passive-aggressive notification where team members announce reckless changes without listening to feedback.
