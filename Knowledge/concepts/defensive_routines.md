# Defensive Routines (Model I Theory)

## Definition

Defensive Routines are habitual individual actions, policies, or systemic patterns designed to prevent people and organizations from experiencing threat, embarrassment, or loss of control, but which simultaneously prevent learning the truth needed to resolve underlying systemic dysfunctions. As formulated by Chris Argyris, Model I defensive routines prioritize unilateral control, face-saving, and the suppression of negative feelings, trapping teams in single-loop learning cycles.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Governing Variables of Model I:**
   - Maintain unilateral control over the environment and task.
   - Maximize winning, minimize losing.
   - Suppress negative or threatening feelings.
   - Appear rational, competent, and unflappable at all times.
2. **Defensive Anger as X-Ray:** When organizational feedback or process change triggers defensive outbursts (*"Stay in your lane," "We already tried that"*), the reaction is not a technical boundary statement—it is a defensive shield protecting an unacknowledged compromised decision or underlying vulnerability.
3. **Double-Loop Resolution:** Do not meet defensive anger with counter-advocacy or tactical escalation. Re-anchor the dialogue to objective system delivery constraints, shared goals, and observable data.

## Counter-Arguments & Data Gaps

1. **Against:** Attributing all managerial resistance or boundary enforcement to unconscious Model I threat-suppression can lead to over-pathologizing legitimate governance, regulatory constraints, or necessary operational boundaries.
2. **Data Gap:** We need empirical tracking on whether content diagnosing executive defensiveness resonates with Senior/Director decision-makers or triggers defensive unfollows/polarization.
3. **Bias Check:** Avoid assuming the agile coach is immune to Model I routines. Coaches frequently retreat into dogmatic process enforcement as their own defensive routine when their recommendations fail.
