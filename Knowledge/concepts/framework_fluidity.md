# Framework Fluidity (No Favorite Weapon)

## Definition

From Musashi's *A Book of Five Rings* (The Book of Ground): "Do not have a favourite weapon." The principle of extreme adaptability to the terrain, rather than forcing a predefined solution onto a complex problem. Becoming over-familiar with one weapon is as much a fault as not knowing it sufficiently well.

## Source Passages (A Book of Five Rings)

> "You should not have a favourite weapon. To become over-familiar with one weapon is as much a fault as not knowing it sufficiently well. You should not copy others, but use weapons which you can handle properly. It is bad for commanders and troopers to have likes and dislikes. These are things you must learn thoroughly."
> — *Book of Ground, "Weapons"*

> "Do not think dishonestly. The Way is in training. Become acquainted with every art. Know the Ways of all professions. Distinguish between gain and loss in worldly matters. Develop intuitive judgement and understanding for everything. Perceive those things which cannot be seen. Pay attention even to trifles. Do nothing which is of no use."
> — *Book of Ground, "The Nine Principles"*

> "The foreman carpenter must know the architectural theory of towers and temples, and the plans of palaces, and must employ men to raise up houses. The Way of the foreman carpenter is the same as the Way of the commander of a warrior house."
> — *Book of Ground, "Comparing Carpentry to Strategy"*

> "The foreman should take into account the abilities and limitations of his men, circulating among them and asking nothing unreasonable. He should know their morale and spirit, and encourage them when necessary. This is the same as the principle of strategy."
> — *Book of Ground, "Comparing Carpentry to Strategy"*

## Scrum Master Application

- **Don't dogmatize Scrum.** The SM who insists on Scrum-by-the-book in every context is wielding a "favourite weapon." Kanban, XP, Lean, and hybrid approaches are all valid tools depending on the terrain.
- **The Carpenter-Commander parallel:** Musashi's foreman carpenter analogy maps directly to the SM role — know the abilities and limitations of your team, circulate among them, ask nothing unreasonable, know their morale. This is facilitation, not command.
- **AI as a weapon, not THE weapon:** This conceptualises Kaan's approach to AI — use it for product discovery when it fits, but don't force it everywhere as the "favourite weapon."
- **"Know the Ways of all professions":** The SM should understand the PO's world, the developer's craft, the business owner's constraints. Not to do their jobs, but to facilitate across boundaries.

## Counter-Arguments & Data Gaps

1. **Against:** "No favourite weapon" could be misread as anti-specialization. Musashi mastered the sword before rejecting attachment to it. The SM must master Scrum before they can fluidly move beyond it. Premature fluidity is just incompetence.
2. **Against:** In organizational contexts, framework consistency has value — teams need shared language and predictable ceremonies. "Fluidity" without a base creates confusion, not adaptability.
3. **Data Gap:** No content published yet. The "Carpenter-Commander" passage is exceptionally strong for SM content but untested with the audience.
4. **Opportunity:** The Carpenter-Commander passage maps so directly to the SM/team relationship that it could anchor its own post — separate from the "no favourite weapon" concept.
